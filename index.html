About

AI that shows its work.

I’m a computer science student at BRAC University who builds AI meant to work outside the lab. Most of my work sits where computer vision meets people who need it to be dependable.

That includes a first-author IEEE Access paper that recognises signs of stroke from facial landmarks and explains each prediction, and an assistive vision agent for blind and low-vision users that decides when to answer, hedge, or ask for a better photo. On the Mongol Tori Mars Rover team I designed PCBs and tuned onboard detection. Lately I build LLM systems too, like a document assistant that cites the page behind every answer. What connects it all: AI that shows its reasoning and admits what it doesn’t know.

Ongoing research

Edge-Native Assistive Vision Agent

An agentic vision assistant for blind and low-vision users. On-device tools answer most questions; a calibrated-competence policy decides when to answer, hedge, ask for a better photo, or decline. A cloud vision-language model is called only when the local tools can’t settle it.

  • 3,000blind-user photo questions in the benchmark
  • 1,358automated tests passing
  • 5tools compared on every question
