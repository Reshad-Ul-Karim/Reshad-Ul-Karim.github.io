Overview

Single images are ambiguous about the physical world behind them. A real corridor, its reflection in a mirror and a photo of it shown on a screen can produce one and the same image, and a model that reads 3D geometry from that image has no way to tell which world it is looking at.

This work asks a sharper question than “what is the depth here?”: can moving the camera settle the scene’s physical explanation at all, and if so, which motion does it?

Approach

The rival explanations of a scene are kept side by side. The system then chooses the camera motion that best separates the closest of them, because real depth, a reflection and a flat display respond to motion in different ways.

Identification is treated as something to earn, not assume: when no available motion can tell the rivals apart, the system abstains instead of reporting a confident geometry that the evidence does not support.
