Reliable Connectome-Based Autism Classification: Calibration and Explanation Stability

How far can brain-connectivity classifiers for autism be trusted? This study looks past accuracy to calibrated probabilities and explanations that stay stable across cohorts and sites.

Overview

Autism classifiers built on brain-connectivity data are often reported by accuracy alone. For anything close to clinical use that is not enough: a model also has to say how sure it is, honestly, and point to the same evidence when the patients or the scanner change.

Approach

The study compares linear and tree-based models on connectome features under controlled cohort and site-level validation, so results reflect generalisation to new sites rather than memorised site effects.

Each model is judged on three things together: how well it classifies, how well its probabilities are calibrated, and how stable its explanations stay across cohorts.
